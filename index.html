AUSTRALIAN CREATIVE INDUSTRIES / DATA STORY

The Shape of Creativity

Exploring the economic value of Australia's creative industries and how Australians engage with the arts.

01 / The Creative Economy

Economic values are reported at current prices and are not adjusted for inflation.

Cultural and creative GDP increased from A$47.8 billion in 2015–16 to A$70.3 billion in 2024–25.

However, its share of Australia's total GDP fell from 2.87% to 2.53% over the same period.

Gross value added (GVA) measures the value of production after subtracting the goods and services used up in the process. Thicker bands show higher GVA.

Specialised activity contributed the most throughout the period. Support and embedded activities increased in value, but their shares were lower in 2024–25 than in 2015–16.

02 / Industry Landscape

Industry size and GDP changes from 2015–16 to 2024–25.

Advertising and print publishing generated 35.7 billion Australian dollars in 2024–25, accounting for 50.8% of creative industry GDP.

The five largest industries are shown separately, with the remaining ten grouped as Other industries.

These seven industries each contributed at least 2 billion Australian dollars in 2015–16 or 2024–25.

Six of the seven industries grew between the two years. Film and television GDP declined from 8.00 billion to 7.30 billion Australian dollars.

Each industry's GDP in 2015–16 is set to 100: 150 means 50% higher than its starting level, while 80 means 20% lower.

From 2015–16 to 2024–25, GDP grew by 83.3% in design and fashion and 62.0% in advertising. The other two industries ended the period below their 2015–16 levels.

Creative GDP rose by A$22.5 billion. Advertising and print publishing accounted for 60.6% of this increase.

The combined decline in film and television and radio was A$867.1 million.
